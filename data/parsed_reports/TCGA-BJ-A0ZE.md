# BACKGROUND:

Mutations in either BRAF or RAS genes or RET/PTC rearrangements are found in more than 70% of papillary thyroid carcinomas. BRAF V600E mutation has been associated with more aggressive behavior of papillary carcinoma. The association between BRAF V600E mutation and features of tumor aggressiveness have also been observed in papillary microcarcinomas. Mutations in the RAS genes occur with lower frequency in oncocytic (Hürthle cell) carcinomas. PAX8/PPARg rearrangement occurs in approximately 70% of follicular thyroid carcinomas. Regarding the specificity of these mutations for cancer, BRAF V600E mutation and RET/PTC rearrangements are overall specific for malignancy in the thyroid, although they have been reported with a very low frequency in benign thyroid lesions. RAS mutations occur in 40-50% of follicular and anaplastic carcinomas, and 30-40% of malignant and benign thyroid tumors, being found in 10-15% of follicular adenomas and papillary carcinomas.

# MUTATIONAL ANALYSIS:

For paraffin-embedded surgical specimens, manual microdissection was performed to collect tumor tissue. Specimens with a minimum of 50% tumor cells in a microdissection target are accepted for the analysis. Optical density readings were obtained. Real-time PCR was performed on the platform to amplify BRAF codons 599-601, NRAS codon 61, HRAS codon 61, and KRAS codons 12/13 sequences. Post-PCR melting curve analysis was used to detect possible mutations. If required, the mutation type DNA from samples positive for each of these mutations was used as positive controls. Amplification at 35 cycles or earlier product on PCR is considered sufficient for the analysis. The limit of detection is approximately 10-20% of alleles with mutation present in the background of normal DNA.

# RET/PTC Methodology Description:

Probe: RET/PTC Probe (10g11.2) and RET/PTC1 Dual-Color Fusion Probe 10q11.2-10q21

Probe Description: The RET probe (labeled SpectrumGreen) is a 207 kb DNA probe located at band 10q11.2. The H4 and 369 probes are approximately 100kb each (labeled in - at band 10q21).

RET/PTC rearrangement is suggested to be present when three RET signals are identified in greater than 8% of the analyzed cells. RET/PTC 1 rearrangement detection is as follows: Normal cells without the orange signals separated. Cells with the RET/PTC1 rearrangement show two pairs of signals (green and orange) and one green and one orange signal separated in greater than 8%.

RET/PTC FISH analysis was manually performed and quantitatively assessed by analysis of a minimum of 60 cells within the targeted area using the RET SpectrumGreen and (369/H4) SpectrumOrange probes.
