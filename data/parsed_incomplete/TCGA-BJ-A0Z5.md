Mutations in either BRAF or RAS genes or RET/PTC rearrangements are found in more than 70% of papillary thyroid carcinomas (1). BRAF mutation has been associated with more aggressive behavior of Papillary carcinoma (2, 3). The association between BRAF V600E mutation and features of tumor aggressiveness have also been observed in papillary microcarcinomas (4). Mutations in the RAS genes or PAX8-PPARg rearrangement occur in -70% of follicular thyroid carcinomas and with lower frequency in oncocytic (Hurthle cell) carcinomas (5). Regarding the specificity of these mutations for cancer, BRAF V600E mutation and RET/PTC and PAX8-PPARg rearrangements are overall specific for malignancy in the thyroid, although they have been reported with a very low frequency in benign thyroid lesions (6). RAS mutations occur in malignant and benign thyroid tumors, being found in -40-50% of follicular and anaplastic carcinomas, 30-40% of follicular adenomas and 10-15% of papillary carcinomas (6).

# SAMPLE PREPARATION AND PROCEDURE

For paraffin-embedded surgical specimens, manual microdissection was performed to collect tumor tissue. Specimens with the minimum of 50% of tumor microdissection target are accepted for the analysis. Optical density readings were obtained. Real-time PCR was performed on the platform to amplify BRAF, NRAS codon 61, HRAS codon 61, and KRAS codons 12113 sequences. Post-PCR melting curve analysis was used to detect possible mutations. If required, the mutation type was confirmed by Sanger sequencing of the PCR product on DNA from samples positive for each of these mutations was used as positive controls. Amplification at 35 cycles or earlier was considered sufficient for the analysis. The limit of detection is approximately 10% of alleles with mutation present in the background of normal DNA and RNA.

# RET/PTC Methodology Description:

Probe: RET/PTC Probe (10811.2) and RETIPTC1 Dual-Color Fusion Probe 10811.2-10g21

Probe Description: The RET probe (labeled) is a 207 kb DNA probe located at band 10811.2. The H4 and 369 probes are approximately 100kb each (labeled in ‑), and are located at band 10g21.

RET/PTC cells rearrangement is suggested to be present when three RET signals are identified in greater than 8% of the analyzed cells. RET/PTC1 rearrangement detection is as follows: Normal cells without the RET/PTC1 rearrangement show two pairs of green and orange signals separated. Cells with the RET/PTC1 rearrangement show two pairs of signals (green and orange) in juxtaposition and one green and one orange signal separated in greater than 8% of analyzed cells within the targeted area.

RET/PTC FISH analysis was manually performed and quantitatively assessed by analysis of a minimum of 60 cells using the (369/H4) SpectrumOrange and the RET SpectrumGreen probes.
