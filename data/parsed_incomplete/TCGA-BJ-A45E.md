# BACKGROUND:

BRAF or RAS genes or RET/PTC rearrangements are found in more than 70% of papillary thyroid carcinomas (1). Mutations in either PAX8-PPARg rearrangement occur in ~70% of follicular thyroid carcinomas (2). Finding of BRAF, RET/PTC or RAS mutation in thyroid FNA samples correlates with malignancy in 74-85% of cases (3,4). Based on our prospective study at 5% risk of cancer in thyroid nodules negative for all of these mutations is 5.9% in nodules with atypia of undetermined significance (AUS) cytology (including the 2-3% risk of invasive cancer and 0.5% risk of cancer with extrathyroidal spread in this group), 14% in nodules with follicular neoplasm/suspicious for follicular neoplasm (FN/SFN) cytology, and 28% in nodules with suspicious for malignant cells (SMC) cytology (5). In addition, BRAF V600E (T1799A) mutation has been associated with more aggressive behavior of papillary carcinoma (6) and may be used for risk stratification of patients with papillary thyroid cancer (7). However, single cases of false-positive BRAF mutation detection in thyroid FNA samples have been reported in the literature (8); therefore, the results of molecular testing should be interpreted in combination with clinical information, imaging, and cytology.

# MUTATIONAL ANALYSIS:

Specimen manual dissection was performed to collect tumor tissue. Specimens with the minimum of 50% tumor cells were accepted for the analysis. Optical density readings were obtained. Real-time PCR was performed on BRAF codons 599-601; NRAS codon 61, HRAS codons 12/13 sequences. Post-PCR analysis was used to detect possible mutations. Sanger sequencing confirmed the mutation type.
